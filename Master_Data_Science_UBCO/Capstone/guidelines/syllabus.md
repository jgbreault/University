## Detailed Description

The Capstone Project provides students with the opportunity to apply the theoretical and practical knowledge gained throughout the Master of Data Science (MDS) program to a real-world, client-focused problem involving real data.

Working in teams, students will collaborate closely with a client to understand the problem, refine research questions and deliverables, and design and implement an appropriate analysis plan. Drawing on tools and techniques learned throughout the program, teams will produce a data-driven solution that makes meaningful progress on the client’s project.

Given the open-ended nature of data science projects, multiple approaches are encouraged. Teams may build on existing work or explore alternative perspectives and methodologies. Open regular and effective communication with the client is an essential and requierd component of the project.

Teams will be required to develop strategies and timelines for working on the project, track and report their progress regularly to the client and teaching mentors. The results of the team’s work will be presented in a written form as well as with a formal presentation at the end of the project, in addition to the possibility of producing other products, such as a web application.

---

## Learning Outcomes

After completing this course, students will be able to:

1. Demonstrate the ability to work as a team to conduct and manage a data science project from research question to final deliverables  
2. Conduct a literature search and summarize current approaches  
3. Translate project objectives into a realistic work plan and statement of work  
4. Effectively communicate project concepts, methods, and results through presentations  
5. Professionally present project outcomes to technical and non-technical audiences  

---

## Student Expectations

Student groups are expected to:
1. Communicate productively, identify sub-problems that could be worked on individually by team members, and integrate contributions of team members into a cohesive final product;
2. Plan and manage their work effectively, including developing timelines, organizing tasks, and maintain regular communication with their client to ensure the project remains aligned with their needs;
3. Acknowledge and correctly cite/attribute sources and assistance;
4. Work at least four full-time days per week (~32 hours) on the project;
5. Document and present their work at UBC (using written, oral, and visual means) at various points throughout the course with all sources and assistance must be appropriately acknowledged.
6. Present weekly progress updates to the teaching team are required.
7. Optionally (but strongly recommended), present their final product and findings to the capstone partner’s organization.

## Weekly Presentations/Checkin Meetings

Weekly meetings with the teaching team are intended to support your project development through regular feedback on your progress and overall approach. These in-person meetings will be scheduled via Canvas and will involve your team (not the full class) meeting with one of the co-instructors (your team mentor).

Meetings will consist of a short team presentation followed by discussion and feedback. Presentations should summarize recent progress, key results, challenges, and next steps.

Detailed expectations and a suggested presentation structure can be found [here](guidelines/weekly-presentations.md).

## Academic Integrity
The academic enterprise is founded on honesty, civility, and integrity.  As members of this enterprise, all students are expected to know, understand, and follow the codes of conduct regarding academic integrity.  At the most basic level, this means submitting only original work done by you and acknowledging all sources of information or ideas and attributing them to others as required.  This also means you should not cheat, copy, or mislead others about what is your work.  Violations of academic integrity (i.e., misconduct) lead to the breakdown of the academic enterprise, and therefore serious consequences arise, and harsh sanctions are imposed.  For example, incidences of plagiarism or cheating may result in a mark of zero on the assignment or exam and more serious consequences may apply if the matter is referred to the President’s Advisory Committee on Student Discipline.  Careful records are kept to monitor and prevent recurrences.  The use of artificial intelligence (AI) assistance for any assessed portions of this course is not permitted.

A more detailed description of academic integrity, including the University’s policies and procedures, may be found in the Academic Calendar at: http://okanagan.students.ubc.ca/calendar/index.cfm?tree=3,54,111,0.
